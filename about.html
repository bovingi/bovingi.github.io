About Me.

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar in the Record Lab in the Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Conservation Biology at the University of Maine. I completed my PhD in Ecology, Evolution, and Marine Biology at the University of California, Santa Barbara in 2026, working in the Landscape Ecophysiology and Function Lab. My dissertation was titled From mechanism to monitoring: ecophysiology for adaptive forest management. Before that, I studied Biology and Environmental History at Reed College.

I am interested in the ways that climate change impacts forested ecosystems, with an emphasis on applications in land management. Using methods and insights from plant physiology, population ecology, fire ecology, and remote sensing, my work aims to build better management tools, inform restoration practices, and further our fundamental understanding of forest dynamics. My projects span drought tolerance and within-species trait variation, the links between plant hydration, flammability and remotely sensed signals, and the physiological limits of conifer seedlings regenerating after wildfire in the Sierra Nevada.

Outside of science, I love long walks on the beach with my dog, Pickle. You can also find me doing ceramics or making other art, attempting to surf, or mountain biking.
